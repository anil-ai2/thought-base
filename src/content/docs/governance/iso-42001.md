## TL;DR

ISO/IEC 42001 is the **certifiable AI management system** standard. Where NIST AI RMF is voluntary guidance, ISO 42001 is auditable and certifiable — the thing you can put on a slide to a board or a regulator and say "we are certified." It is the governance credibility anchor for an architect targeting regulated enterprises.

## What it is

- An **AI management system (AIMS)** standard, structured like ISO 27001 (Plan-Do-Check-Act).
- Covers: AI policy, roles and responsibilities, risk assessment, AI system impact assessment, data management, third-party/supplier management, monitoring, continual improvement.
- **Annex A** lists specific AI controls (data, model, transparency, human oversight, etc.) that can be selected based on risk.
- Certifiable by an accredited body; integrates with ISO 27001/27701 if you already have them.

## Why it matters for this seat

- **Exec trust:** "ISO 42001 certified" is a phrase boards and regulators understand instantly.
- **Audit language:** the standard's vocabulary (AIMS, SoA, internal audit, management review) is the language external auditors speak.
- **Differentiation:** few AI architects hold the Lead Implementer credential; it signals depth beyond tooling.
- **Reusable:** the AIMS is the operating model in standards form — it maps directly to the [Transformation Operating Model](/playbooks/operating-model/) and the [Control Framework](/governance/control-framework/).

## ISO 42001 vs. NIST AI RMF

| Dimension | NIST AI RMF | ISO 42001 |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Voluntary guidance | Certifiable standard |
| Cost to adopt | Free | Audit fees, training |
| Structure | Functions (Govern/Map/Measure/Manage) | Management system (PDCA) + Annex A controls |
| Audience | Engineers, risk teams | Boards, regulators, auditors |
| Best use | Backbone of control library | Credibility anchor and audit target |

Use both: NIST as the working backbone, ISO 42001 as the certification and audit target.

## Certification path (for the architect)

1. **Foundation course** (2–3 days) to learn vocabulary and structure.
2. **Lead Implementer** course + exam — the credential that matters for this seat.
3. **Optional: Lead Auditor** if you want to audit others (useful for consulting, less needed in-house).
4. **Practice:** implement an AIMS on one AI system in your own portfolio (even a small one) before claiming expertise.

Time budget: ~60–90 days part-time alongside other work.

## Implementation in an enterprise (high level)

1. **Scope:** which AI systems, which business units, which jurisdictions.
2. **Gap assessment:** current practice vs. standard requirements.
3. **AI policy & objectives:** board-approved, measurable.
4. **Risk assessment + AI system impact assessment** for each in-scope system.
5. **Select Annex A controls** based on risk; document in a Statement of Applicability (SoA).
6. **Implement controls** (this is where the Control Framework and Control Library plug in).
7. **Internal audit** + management review.
8. **Certification audit** (Stage 1 document review, Stage 2 on-site).
9. **Continual improvement** — surveillance audits annually.

## Common pitfalls

- Starting with Annex A controls before defining scope and risk assessment — you end up with a control set that doesn't match your actual AI.
- Treating ISO 42001 as a documentation exercise. Auditors test whether controls *operate*, not whether they are written.
- Ignoring integration with existing ISO 27001/27701 programs — duplicating work and annoying the security team.
- Chasing the certificate before the substance. The artifact (a working AIMS on one system) is worth more than the paper.
