## TL;DR

The "translate, don't cite" rule in action: one-line answers for the regulations you will be asked about most, each containing a control, evidence and metric — no article numbers. Memorize these; they are worth comp.

## GDPR Art. 5 (data minimization)

> "We enforce data minimization as code — every pipeline deploy is scanned, and 0 PII has reached non-prod in 12 months."

## GDPR Art. 15–22 (data subject rights)

> "Rights are an API call, not a project — erasure cascades automatically and we report fulfillment SLA monthly."

## GDPR Art. 25 (protection by design)

> "Privacy is reviewed before build, not after — every project with personal data has a DPIA gate before production."

## EU AI Act (high-risk, Art. 9–15)

> "No high-risk AI ships without a model card, an eval pass and a named human approver — enforced by the platform, not by policy documents."

## RBI (banking AI governance)

> "Every AI use case is registered, risk-tiered and gated by the platform — regulator-ready evidence on demand."

## DPDP Act (India)

> "Consent and purpose are metadata on every record — erasure is an API call, not a project."

## NIST AI RMF

> "We manage AI risk through four functions — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage — each with named owners and evidence."

## ISO 42001

> "We run an auditable AI management system, not a binder — certified, with internal audits and management reviews."

## SEBI (threat modeling)

> "Every AI system in scope is threat-modeled and security-reviewed before production."

## How to use

1. Memorize the 3–4 most relevant to your target vertical.
2. Practice the "cite-and-translate" drill daily: one regulation → legal version → exec version with metric (10 min/day, 30 days).
3. Add your own as you encounter new regulations; keep the format (control + evidence + metric, no article numbers).
4. Use in interviews, steering committees and regulator conversations.
